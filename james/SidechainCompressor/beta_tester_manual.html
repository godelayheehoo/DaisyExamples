What this thing does

It's a stereo compressor with a dedicated sidechain input. Plug your stereo mix (or a synth, or a bus) into the main in, plug a kick drum (or any other trigger signal) into the sidechain input, and it'll duck/compress the stereo signal in time with whatever's hitting the sidechain in. Classic "pumping" effect, but with a lot of control over how musical or aggressive it sounds.

Under the hood it's an Electrosmith Daisy Seed — a little embedded computer doing all the audio processing in real time. Almost everything happens in software, which means if something's off I can usually just fix it and send you a firmware update rather than needing the unit back (see the firmware section below).

Still a beta It works, but it's still evolving — components, noise floor, firmware, all of it might change before this is "done." Poke at it, push it past where you'd normally stop, and tell me what happens. I'd much rather hear "this broke" than have you go easy on it.

Powering it up

  • Normal use: just the 9V barrel jack on the back. That's it — no USB needed day to day. Plug it in and it boots straight into the effect.
  • Polarity: center-negative — it's also labeled on the box, so just match that if you're ever using a different supply.
  • USB-C port: only needed if I send you a firmware update. Don't need it plugged in otherwise, and don't need it plugged in alongside the 9V for normal playing.
  • If it doesn't power on, double check the polarity above before anything else — there's reverse-polarity protection in there too, so worst case it just stays off rather than anything getting damaged.

Connections

  • Audio In (stereo, 1/4" TRS): the signal you want compressed — a mix, a synth, a drum bus, whatever.
  • Audio Out (stereo, 1/4" TRS): the processed (or bypassed) output.
  • Sidechain In (mono, 1/4" TS): the trigger signal — usually a kick drum, but anything with a clear transient works. This is what drives the ducking, it is not itself heard in the output.
  • 9V barrel jack: main power.
  • USB-C: firmware updates only.
Mono in/out If you run mono in and mono out instead of stereo, it should still work — a mono plug in a stereo (TRS) jack should just feed the left channel, so you'd only hear/process the left side. Width is the one knob I'm genuinely not sure about in that situation — it's designed to spread a stereo signal, so with only one channel actually carrying audio it might do nothing, or it might do something a little weird. Curious what you actually hear if you try it.

Knobs & what they do

Nine knobs total. Ranges below are approximate — part of what I want your ears on is whether these ranges feel right.

LabelNameRangeWhat it does
TThreshold−60 dB to 0 dBThe level the sidechain envelope has to cross before compression kicks in. Fully counter-clockwise = compresses almost everything; fully clockwise = only the loudest hits trigger it.
AlphaRatio1:1 → 100:1, then 1:∞How hard the gain gets pulled down once you're past threshold. Past roughly the last 5% of the knob's travel, it jumps to a hard limit (infinite ratio) — everything above threshold gets clamped.
AAttack~0.2 ms – 150 msHow fast the compression grabs once threshold is crossed. Fast = clamps the transient itself; slow = lets the transient through and only catches the sustain.
RRelease~0.2 ms – 500 msHow fast the compression lets go once the signal drops back below threshold. Fast = choppy/pumpy; slow = smoother, more glued.
MMix0 – 100%Blend of dry (unprocessed) vs. wet (compressed) signal. Useful for parallel-compression-style effects at lower settings.
DDrive / Ducking Depth0 – 100%Dual-purpose, depends on the character switch position — see below. In Soft/Wavefold modes it's a saturation amount; in Ducker mode it's how deep the duck goes.
WWidth0 – 200%Stereo width of the output. 100% = untouched. Below narrows toward mono, above widens.
CCutoff20 Hz – 20 kHzFilters the sidechain trigger signal before it drives the envelope — this shapes what the compressor "listens to," not the main audio. Works together with the L/B/H switch below.
OOutput0 – +24 dBMakeup gain, applied last, after everything else.

Switches

Filter type — L / B / H

Three-position switch that sets what kind of filter is applied to the sidechain trigger signal (in combination with the Cutoff knob above). This only affects what triggers the compression — it does not filter the main stereo audio.

  • L — Low-pass. Only lets frequencies below the Cutoff point through to the envelope follower. Good for isolating a kick fundamental and ignoring cymbals/hi-hats bleeding into the trigger signal.
  • B — Band-pass (center position). Passes a band around the Cutoff frequency.
  • H — High-pass. Only lets frequencies above Cutoff through. Useful if your trigger source is something brighter than a kick.

Character switch — D / W / S

Three-position switch that changes the fundamental behavior of the effect, not just a tone shift. This interacts directly with the Drive knob above.

  • S — Soft. Standard musical compression, with soft/tape-like saturation stacked on afterward. Drive knob = saturation amount.
  • W — Wavefold. Same compression, but the post-compression coloration is wavefolding instead of soft saturation — more complex, synthy, harmonically aggressive. Drive knob = fold amount.
  • D — Ducker. Different animal — see the "Ducker mode" section below. Drive knob becomes Ducking Depth instead of a saturation amount.

Footswitch — bypass

Latching footswitch. Down/engaged = effect active. Up/disengaged = clean pass-through, unprocessed signal. The red LED (below) tracks this.

Ramp Rate pot & Ramp Button

These two controls work together as an LFO auto-mapping system — see the Ramp section below for full details. The short version: hold the Ramp Button, move a target pot to set the sweep range, then turn the Rate pot to dial in the LFO speed, then release. Any of the nine main knobs can be mapped this way.

The LEDs

  • Red LED (bypass indicator): solid on when the effect is engaged, off when bypassed.
  • Pink LED (sidechain activity): pulses in time with whatever's hitting the sidechain input, whenever the envelope crosses your Threshold setting. Only active while the effect is engaged (not lit in bypass). This is your at-a-glance "is it triggering" indicator.
  • Ramp LED (next to the Ramp Button): used only during the LFO learn gesture and as a "pot override" warning — see the Ramp section for what each blink pattern means. Off during normal operation.
Worth flagging Both LEDs run off 3.3V so they're a bit dim by design — curious if that's actually a problem in practice or not. If you find yourself squinting to tell if the pink one's pulsing, especially in a bright room, let me know.

How the sidechain actually works

Whatever you plug into the Sidechain In jack goes through a small analog conditioning circuit before it ever reaches the Daisy's brain, then gets filtered (L/B/H + Cutoff) and turned into an envelope — basically a running measurement of "how loud is this trigger signal right now." That envelope is what drives the compressor's gain reduction on your main stereo signal. The sidechain signal itself is never mixed into the audio output — it's control-only.

Practically: patch a kick drum in, and every time it hits, the compressor's gain reduction kicks in on your main signal per your Threshold/Ratio/Attack/Release settings, then releases before the next hit (or doesn't, if Release is set long — that's a valid creative choice too).

Ramp / LFO auto-mapping

Any of the nine main knobs can be mapped to an internal triangle-wave LFO that automatically sweeps the parameter back and forth between two positions you set. You can map multiple knobs at once — each gets its own independent LFO. Ramp state lives in RAM only; a power cycle clears everything (intentional — it's a performance tool, not a preset system).

Assigning a ramp — the learn gesture

  1. Hold the Ramp Button (the small button next to the Rate knob). The system is now watching for a pot to move.
  2. Move the pot you want to automate — turn it from where you want the LFO's low point to be toward its high point. The Ramp LED lights solid when the target is detected.
  3. Turn the Rate knob to set the LFO speed. The Ramp LED starts blinking at the speed you're dialing in as a live preview. Slow = long, lazy sweeps; fast = tremolo-style flutter.
  4. Release the Ramp Button to commit. The LFO kicks in immediately and the LED goes off.
Safe by default If you release the button before you've moved a target pot, or before you've touched the Rate knob, the gesture cancels cleanly — nothing gets mapped. You have to reach the rate-setting step before releasing commits anything.

Clearing a ramp

Hold the Ramp Button and press the Footswitch once. This clears the most recently assigned ramp. To clear multiple ramps, repeat the gesture — each time it removes the last one assigned. The footswitch doesn't toggle bypass during this gesture.

The Ramp LED: what each pattern means

  • Solid on: target pot detected, you're in the "set high point" phase — keep holding the button and touch the Rate knob next.
  • Blinking: you're in the rate-setting phase — the blink rate mirrors the LFO speed you're currently dialing in. Release the button to commit at this speed.
  • Triple flash (three quick blinks, then off): outside of learn mode, this fires when you physically turn a knob that's currently being driven by a ramp. It's a reminder that the pot is overridden — the LFO is still running and will continue to. To stop it you need to clear the ramp (hold Ramp Button + tap Footswitch).
  • Off: everything idle, no learn gesture in progress, no active ramps being touched.

Rate range

The Rate knob sweeps from approximately 0.02 Hz (one full cycle every ~50 seconds — barely perceptible movement) to 8 Hz (fast tremolo). The mapping is exponential, so the lower end of the knob covers slow/musical rates and the last quarter covers the faster, more rhythmic range.

Ducker mode vs. Soft / Wavefold

Soft and Wavefold modes are both "real" compressors — proportional gain reduction shaped by Threshold/Ratio/Attack/Release, with a color stage stacked on top. Ducker mode is a different, more binary effect: instead of proportional gain reduction plus coloration, the Drive knob directly sets how deep the ducking cut goes (0 to −100 dB) whenever the sidechain triggers. Think classic sidechain "pump" effect — less about musical compression curves, more about a hard rhythmic drop in level synced to the trigger. Same Threshold/Attack/Release controls still shape when and how fast it happens; Drive just controls how far down it goes instead of how much saturation gets added.

Firmware updates (remote)

There's a small button on the back of the enclosure. That's the bootloader button — it puts the Daisy into a mode where it'll accept new firmware over USB instead of running the current program. You'll only need this if I send you an updated .bin file to fix something or add a feature.

To install an update I send you:

  1. Disconnect the 9V power supply. Power the unit from USB-C only for this process.
  2. Plug the USB-C cable into the unit and your computer, using Chrome (this only works in Chrome/Chromium-based browsers).
  3. Hold the back-panel button down, then plug in USB-C while still holding it (or hold it and tap reset if the unit's already connected) — this drops it into bootloader/DFU mode.
  4. In Chrome, go to flash.daisy.audio.
  5. Go to the File Upload tab, and select the .bin file I sent you.
  6. Click Flash / Program, and wait for it to finish — don't unplug mid-flash.
  7. Once it's done, unplug USB-C, reconnect the 9V supply, and it should boot normally into the new firmware.
Heads up The web programmer has a known quirk where it can cache an old version of a .bin file if you re-upload one with the same filename after I send a revision — if the update doesn't seem to have taken effect, try a hard refresh of the page or re-downloading the file before re-flashing.

If the unit isn't showing up in the browser as a DFU/bootloader device at all, double check you're on Chrome, on a data-capable USB-C cable (not charge-only), and that you actually got it into bootloader mode in step 3 before opening the page.

Ideas for things to look at

  • Whether the knob ranges (especially Attack/Release/Ratio) feel musically useful across their full sweep, or if there's dead zone at either end.
  • LED visibility — see the note above.
  • Any noise, hum, or whine — and under what conditions (which power source, sidechain connected or not, which character mode).
  • How the three character modes (S/W/D) feel relative to each other — too subtle, too different, anything feel broken or unexpectedly harsh?
  • Whether the sidechain filter (L/B/H + Cutoff) is intuitive to dial in with a real kick drum, or fiddly.
  • Anything that seems to just not work as described here.
And honestly — anything else Don't feel limited to the list above. If something feels off, or missing, or you have an idea for a mode/control/behavior you'd want, I want to hear it. This thing's still soft enough in its design that real suggestions can actually go somewhere.

Troubleshooting

  • No sound at all: check the footswitch/red LED — if it's off, you're likely just bypassed. Check the 9V connection.
  • Compressor never triggers: confirm something's actually plugged into the Sidechain In and check the pink LED — if it's never pulsing, try turning Threshold counter-clockwise and confirming your source is hot enough.
  • Weird pumping/level jumps with nothing plugged into the sidechain jack: some jacks can behave oddly when powered via USB alone without the 9V supply connected — if you're seeing this, try switching to 9V power and see if it goes away, and let me know either way.
  • Unit won't power on: see the polarity note under Powering It Up.
  • Anything else weird: just tell me what you were doing when it happened — exact knob positions if you can, which mode, what was plugged in. More detail is always more useful than less.
